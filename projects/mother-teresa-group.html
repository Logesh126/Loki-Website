01 / Overview

A group of institutions, expressed through individual experiences.

The work covered eight educational institutions under the Mother Teresa Group. Each website needed to communicate its own courses, campus life and institutional character, while remaining recognisably part of the same group.

I worked across the UI/UX design of eight separate websites, from page structure and content hierarchy through to high-fidelity desktop interfaces. Alongside the websites, I also created graphic design collateral for institutional communication, admissions and awareness campaigns.

The design approach was to give each institution a clear visual voice rather than force every website into one identical template. Shared interaction patterns and consistent layout principles helped the collection feel connected.

The central design challenge: create eight recognisable institutional experiences without losing the consistency and trust of the parent group.

Across the websites, the shared design principles included consistent navigation behaviour, reusable interface patterns, clear typography hierarchy, structured spacing, accessible contrast and predictable page layouts. The colour and content treatment then gave each institution its own identity.

04 / Design process

From information structure to interface.

A repeatable process helped keep the eight websites coherent while allowing the content and visual direction to change with each institution.

STEP 01

Understand

Review institutional content, programme information and communication priorities.

STEP 02

Structure

Organise navigation, page hierarchy and key information for each website.

STEP 03

Design

Develop the visual direction and high-fidelity desktop interfaces in Figma.

STEP 04

Adapt

Design responsive mobile layouts alongside the desktop experience.

06 / Reflection

Designing for a wider ecosystem.

Working across eight websites required more than repeating a layout. It meant understanding how different institutions communicate, what information matters to their audiences and where consistency should be shared.

The project strengthened my ability to work across multiple related digital experiences, establish reusable design principles and create distinct visual directions within a shared brand ecosystem.

It also connected my UI/UX practice with graphic design — carrying the institution's communication across websites, brochures, admission creatives and awareness material.
